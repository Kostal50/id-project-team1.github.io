We also went through the other questions listed in the script:

Attention and the Wandering Mind

We observed what we were doing, before we reached for our phones. We noticed that a lot of the time, we were waiting for something (the bus, train, a person) and the phone was used to “skip time”. We also noticed that we also reached for our phones mid conversations (face to face or in a call) to either check the time or read a message that was sent to us (either by the person we were having a conversation with or somebody else).

Designing Around Attention

We looked through the apps we use daily and tried every feature to see where effort has been moved onto us. We noticed that a lot of apps were designed to have as little as effort for us as possible, so it was difficult to find a fitting example. What we decided on was a feature from the music-app tidal: if you want to add a song to your playlist, you have to select the playlist yourself. For the first song you want to add in this “session”, it is logical, but after that first song, you still have to select the playlist each time. The reason for that might be, that if you want to select another playlist, you don’t have the “struggle” of deleting the song from the wrong playlist and then adding it to the right one.
